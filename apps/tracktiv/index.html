Tracktiv · for macOS 14 and later

You didn't lose the day.
Here is where it went.

Tracktiv is a time tracker for people who keep three projects going at once and bill none of them to anybody. It records your working day on its own and hands it back at the end of it — so at seven in the evening there is an answer instead of a blank.

Nothing to remember

The day arrives already written

There is no timer to start and nothing to fill in afterwards. Tracktiv watches which app and which window you are working in, and by the evening the list has names on it, breaks in it, and the calls in the right place.

A different day, activity by activity — two hours of a game before lunch, the client all afternoon, and every break in between

It knows one project from another

Not just "Xcode" — the window title says which project is open in it. One editor can drive a different activity for every job you have on.

A break is a break

Leave the Mac and the activity ends by itself. Come back and a new one starts. Lunch never quietly becomes two hours of work.

A call is a call

The moment your camera or microphone goes live the activity becomes Meeting, and when the call ends the work you were on picks up again.

An evening off looks like one

A game in front of you starts a Gaming activity named after the game. The hours you did not work are worth seeing too.

You write these once — an app, and a piece of its window title. After that the labelling is Tracktiv's job, and a rule can switch straight away or ask first.

Three projects at once

Which one actually moved today?

This is the question the evening turns on. Not "was I busy" — you know you were busy — but whether the thing you meant to finish got any of it.

Tracktiv adds the day up per activity and per category, so five scattered stretches on the same project show up as one number. The bar across the top is the whole day in proportion: how much was your own work, how much was the client's, how much was neither.

  • Categories carry your colours. Every activity is filed under exactly one, and the charts add up by kind of work rather than by name.
  • Rename a category and your history follows. The change reaches back through everything already recorded.
  • Nothing gets rounded. The numbers here are the measured ones, to the second.

The other side

What was actually in front of you

Alongside your own labels, Tracktiv keeps the app side of the same hours: how long each application was the one you were looking at, day by day.

It is the less flattering view, and the more useful one. Five hours on the project, yes — and half an hour of mail inside them, in pieces you never noticed.

Select an activity and the list narrows to the apps that were in front of you during it. Click an application and its stretches light up red in the graph above, so you can see exactly where the half hour went.

The longer view

A month you can read in one look

Every day is a ring, and the ring fills as the day does. Your own working day length is the mark it is measured against — past it, the ring turns red.

You do not have to read a single number to see it: the heavy weeks are the solid green ones, the days that ran long carry red, and the quiet Saturdays are barely a stroke. Patterns you would never notice from inside them turn up here — the fortnight that quietly became eleven-hour days, the two weekends that were not weekends.

The same ring sits in the menu bar all day. A glance at it tells you how much of your working day is gone, without opening anything.

Yours to correct

Got it wrong? Fixing it takes a second

Automatic tracking is right most of the time, not all of it. So the record is yours to edit: click the menu bar icon, rename what it called the last hour, move it to another category, split it in two where the work actually changed, or merge the three pieces it made of one job.

⌘Z takes back anything you did not mean, and the week above the list tells you which day to open next.

  • The running activity is editable while it runs. A keyboard shortcut puts the cursor straight in its name.
  • Breaks are part of the record. A day of solid work and a day of five restarts are not the same day, and it shows.
  • Start something by hand any time. Automatic tracking is the default, not the only way in.

Beyond the window

Talk your week over with something that has the numbers

Tracktiv ships with a small command line tool and a skill in the open Agent Skills format, which Claude Code, Codex and many other assistants read. Ask how the week went and the answer is built from measured time, not from what you happen to remember.

It writes as well as reads, so the tedious corrections can be spoken instead of clicked — and every change lands on the same undo stack as the app's own editing.

  • "How did my week go, and what should I change?"
  • "Rename this morning's activities to Client work."
  • "I forgot to stop tracking at lunch — fix Tuesday."

Privacy

A record this personal should not go anywhere

A time tracker sees your whole working day. That is exactly why this one was built without the means to send any of it anywhere.

No network, by construction

Tracktiv is not built with the network entitlement, so macOS would refuse to let it reach the internet even if it tried. No account, no server, no sync, no analytics.

The title, not the screen

It asks macOS what the frontmost window is called and looks at nothing else. The one exception is a trigger you set up for an app that reports no title — then it reads that title bar, on your Mac, and stores neither the image nor the text.

Nobody is watching over you

There is no team dashboard, no manager view and no shared report. Tracktiv is written for the person using it, and it has no second audience.

Reviewed by Apple

Tracktiv ships only through Apple: TestFlight now, the Mac App Store later. Every build is reviewed and its permissions are on record — you do not have to take our word for it.

And a few more things

Small, and worth having

Categories are yours to name, colour and pick a symbol for, and the symbol turns up everywhere that category's time does — in the list, in the graph, in the totals.

  • Keyboard, not mouse. Global shortcuts open the day, start a new activity, or drop the cursor into the name of the one that is running.
  • It tells you when it switched. A notification whenever Tracktiv changed the activity instead of you. The switches you make yourself pass without a word.
  • Four languages. English, German, Hungarian and Slovak, following whatever your Mac is set to.
  • It stays out of the way. No Dock icon, no window in your way, no daily nag.
